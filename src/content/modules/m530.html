1. Introduction

This unit is involved with electric current. After defining electric current and discussing the nature of electric current, the electric current density \(\vec{J}(\vec{r})\) is defined. The electric current density \(\vec{J}(\vec{r})\) so defined is another vector field which enters directly into the differential equations of electromagnetic theory, that is, Maxwell's equations. One of these differential equations, the equation of continuity, which is essentially a statement of the conservation of electric charge, relates the current density \(\vec{J}(\vec{r},t)\) and the charge density \(\rho(\vec{r},t)\).

\[\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \Grad \cdot \vec{J} = 0\,.\]

Notice that an explicit time dependence is included in the current and charge density.

4. Supplementary Notes

4a. Problem 7-3


At steady state, \(\partial\rho/\partial t = 0 \Longrightarrow \Grad \cdot \vec{J} = 0\). This condition on \(\vec{J}\) can be used to show that the normal component of \(\vec{J}\) is continuous across the interface. So,

\[J_{1n} = J_{2n}\,.\]

The proof is the same as outlined in Sec. 4-7 of the text for the displacement vector \(\vec{D}\). From Ohm's law, \(\Grad \cdot \vec{J} = 0 \Longrightarrow \Grad \cdot \vec{E} = 0\). At steady state, \(\vec{E} = - \Grad V\) so that \(\Grad \cdot \vec{E} = 0 \Longrightarrow \nabla^2 V = 0\). So \(V\) can be written as:

\[V_1(x) = \alpha_1 x + \beta_1\,,\]

\[V_2(x) = \alpha_2 x + \beta_2\,,\]

where

\[V_1(x = 0) = V_1\,, \qquad V_1(x = a) = V_2(x = a) = V_3\,,\]

\[V_2(x = d) = V_2\,.\]

Thus,

\[\begin{aligned}V_1 & = \beta_1\,, \\ V_3 & = \alpha_1 a + V_1 = \alpha_2 a + \beta_2 \\ V_2 & = \alpha_2 d + \beta_2\,.\end{aligned}\]

These equations can be easily solved for \(\alpha_1\), \(\alpha_2\), \(\beta_1\) and \(\beta_2\) to get:

\[V_1(x)=\frac{V_3-V_1}{a} x + V_1\,,\]

\[V_2(x)=\frac{V_2-V_3}{d-a} x + V_3\,.\]

Thus,

\[E_1(x)=-\frac{V_3-V_1}{a}; \qquad E_2(x)=-\frac{V_2-V_3}{d-a}\,.\]

But, \(J_{1n} = g_1 E_1\) and \(J_{2n} = g_2 E_2\). So, Eqs. (1) and Eq. (2) give:

\[\begin{aligned}g_1 E_1 & = g_2 E_2\,, \\ g_1\frac{V_3-V_1}{a} & = g_2\frac{V_2-V_3}{d-a}\,,\end{aligned}\]

\[\Longrightarrow V_3 = \frac{V_1 g_1 (d-a) + V_2 g_2a} {g_1 (d-a) + g_2 a}\,.\]

You can calculate the free charge density on the interface from

\[D_{1n} - \,D_{2n} = \sigma\,.\]
