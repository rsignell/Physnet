The responses to the three questions are to be cross-correlated and free-form post-interviews to be conducted. .PG .SC THE COMPLEX PLANE AGAIN: SOLUTION!__________________________________ .PA A smoothly animated sequence we had made for the complex plane series had seemed oddly ineffective for instructional purposes. After puzzling over this for some time, it occurred to me that the problem was that there was too much information to keep track of in the constantly-changing images. Of the available options, the best seemed to be cut down on the number of frames presented to the viewer. .PA We filmed the basic complex-plane sequence again, now using only every 48th frame. Each frame was held on the screen for 1 second, then lap-dissolved into the next frame over a period of 1 second. Thus the net rate of movement remained the same as with smooth animation, but now viewers said that they had time to absorb and compare the changes. Furthermore, the dissolves produced a beautiful illusion of motion. Every time I have presented this lap-dissolved sequence to an audience they have made appreciative sounds as two poles approach each other, merge into one, and then separate again and move off in new directions. .PA The lap-dissolve sequence was made with the inexpensive old checkerboard algorithm since we no longer needed the micro-accuracy necessary for smooth animation. The cost was further reduced, of course, by the need to plot a far smaller number of frames. .SC PROPOSAL FOR THE COMPLEX PLANE SERIES_____________________________________ .PA We now propose to make the complex plane films utilizing the lap-dissolve technique to simulate motion.
