Relativistic Momentum: Particle Decays (Peter Signell)

⏱ 1 hour(s)

Learning Objectives

Input Skills

  • 1. Work simple conservation of momentum problems MISN-0-14.
  • 2. Use the relativistic form for total energy in conservation of energy problems MISN-0-23.
  • 3. Expand the square root function in a Taylor series about a given point MISN-0-4.

Output Skills (Knowledge)

  • K1. Reduce the expression for relativistic momentum to its non-relativistic form, using the general expression for Taylor's Series for the expansion of a function about a point.
  • K2. Show that \(\vec{F}\,=\,m\vec{a}\) is generally valid only for \(v^2\,\ll\,c^2\).

Output Skills (Problem Solving)

  • S1. Given a particle's rest mass and velocity, calculate its relativistic momentum and energy.
  • S2. Use conservation of energy and momentum to work decay problems, 1 particle \(\Rightarrow\) 2 particles, in the center of mass frame.

2. Work These Problems

2a. Problem A

The rest mass of a π+ (“pi-plus”) meson is about 140 MeV/c2. If a π+ is traveling at 0.8 c, compute its energy in  MeV and momentum in  MeV/c.

2b. Problem B

In the decay of a Λ0 (“lambda-zero”) particle, at rest, to a neutron (n) and a π0 (“pi-zero”), use conservation of energy and momentum to show that the momentum of the neutron is 104 MeV/c.

mass of Λ0=1115.63 MeV/c2
mass of n=939.5656 MeV/c2
mass of π0=134.9739 MeV/c2

  • Note: \(\text{(momentum)}^2 = k^2 v^2 m_0^2 = (k^2 - 1) m_0^2 c^2\) is a useful identity that you can easily prove.
  • Note: For further decay problems see, “Review of Particle Properties,” Physics Letters, Vol. 239, April 12, 1990.

Answers to Problems

  1. A. 233 MeV, 187 MeV/c.
  2. B. Consv. of \(E\): \(m_\bLambda = k_\bn m_\bn + k_\mpi m_\mpi\)

    Consv. of \(\vec{p}\): \(0 = k_\bn \vec{v}_\bn m_\bn + k_\mpi \vec{v}_\mpi m_\mpi\)

    Squaring the second equation and using the identity in the Note,

    (a) \((k_\bn^2 - 1) m_\bn^2 = (k_\mpi^2 - 1) m_\mpi^2\).

    Squaring the first equation after solving it for \(k_\mpi m_\mpi\):

    (b) \(k_\mpi^2 m_\mpi^2 = m_\bLambda^2 - 2 m_\bLambda k_\bn m_\bn + k_\bn^2 m_\bn^2\).

    Then putting (b) into (a) to eliminate \(k_\mpi\) gives:

    \[k_\bn = \frac{m_{\bLambda}^2 - m_{\mpi}^2 + m_\bn^2} {2 m_{\bLambda} m_\bn}\,.\]

    Again using the identity we find:

    \[\text{(momentum)}_\bn = \left( k_\bn^2 - 1 \right)^{1/2} m_\bn c = 103.9\,\text{MeV/c}\,.\]

Problem Supplement

Note: Problems 1, 3, and 4 also occur on this module's Model Exam.

  1. 1. The rest mass of a Σ+ (“sigma-plus”) particle is 1189.37 MeV/c2. If it is traveling at speed 0.8000 c, compute its energy in  MeV and its momentum in  MeV/c.
  2. 2. Reduce the expression for relativistic momentum to its non-relativistic form, using the general expression for Taylor's Series for the expansion of a function about a point.
  3. 3. In the decay of a Σ+ particle at rest, to a proton and a π0 particle, calculate the momentum of the proton in  MeV/c. \[\begin{aligned}m_\bp & = & 938.2592\unit{MeV/c^{2}} \\ m_{\mpi^{0}} & = & 134.9645\unit{MeV/c^{2}} \\ m_{\bSigma^{+}} & = & 1189.37 \unit{MeV/c^{2}} \\\end{aligned}\] Compare your answer to the (189 MeV/c) listed in “Review of Particle Properties,” Physics Letters, Vol. 239, April 12, 1990.
  4. 4. Show that \(\vec{F} = m \vec{a}\) is generally valid only for \(v^2 \ll c^2\).

Brief Answers

  1. 1. 1982 MeV, 1586 MeV/c.
  2. 2. Taylor's Series:

    \(f(x)=f(0) + \frac{f'(0)}{1!} x + \frac{f}{2!} x^2 + \ldots\)

    \(k(x) = (1 - x)^{-1/2}\) where \(x \equiv v^2/c^2\) for our case

    \(k'(x) = (1/2)(1 - x)^{-3/2}\)

    \(k''(x) = (3/4)(1 - x)^{-5/2}\)

    \(k(x) = 1 + (1/2)x + (3/8)x^2 + \ldots\)

    \(k(v^2) = 1 + (1/2)(v^2/c^2) + (3/8)(v^4/c^4) + \ldots\)

    \(\text{mom.} = k v m_0 = m_0 v + (1/2) m_0 (v^3 / c^2) + \ldots\)

    Then if \(v^2 \ll c^2\) we can neglect the second term and get: \[\text{mom.} = m_0 v, \qquad \text{for } v^2 \ll c^2.\]

  3. 3. As in Problem B, derive: \[k_p = \frac{m_\bSigma^2 - m_{\mpi}^2 + m_\bp^2}{2 m_{\bSigma} m_bp}\] and find the numerical value of the momentum.
  4. 4. Newton's Second Law, verified relativistically, is: \[\vec{F} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt},\] but then: \[\begin{aligned}\vec{F} & = \frac{d(m\vec{v})}{dt} \\ & = m \vec{a} + \vec{v} \frac{dm}{dt}, \qquad \text{where } m \equiv k m_0 \\ & \neq m \vec{a}, \text{ unless } v^2 \ll c^2 \text{ so } dm/dt = 0\,.\end{aligned}\]
